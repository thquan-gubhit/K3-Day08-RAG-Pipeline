Welcome and Greetings  
Dear RMIT Vietnam Parents and Families,
We are delighted to welcome you and your students to the RMIT
community.
Navigating university life can be both exciting and challenging for students,
and we understand it can be an anxious time for parents. RMIT is
committed to providing resources and services to ensure a safe and
supportive learning environment for all students.

a partnership between students, families, and the university. As we
empower students to take charge of their success, your support is crucial in

challenges, and proactively seek support from the University when needed.

created by the RMIT Vietnam Family Connect team to foster students’
independence while providing families with the right information and
guidance to support students in being ready for what’s next.
We look forward to building a strong partnership with you and celebrating
your students' remarkable success.

Partnering with RMIT

Roles of parents and families  
As parents and families, your support is invaluable in helping your student navigate their
university journey. While RMIT upholds strict privacy guidelines, you can still play a
crucial role by maintaining open communication with your student, encouraging their
independence, and guiding them on accessing relevant RMIT resources, services and
support.   
Foster student autonomy
Empowerment from parents is crucial to
students’ independence and resilience. By
fostering students’ sense of responsibility,
decision-making abilities, and problem-
solving skills, you are helping to prepare your
student for their future work and life.
Be a source of trust, encouragement and
motivation
Be attentive listeners and trusted mentors or
coaches when needed. Maintain open
communication without judgment so that your
student feels trusted and connected to share
their experiences with you.   
Know university resources
RMIT offers a wide range of free student
resources, services, and support. Familiarise
yourself with these resources and encourage
your student to actively use them.
Recognise the changes and challenges
Your student is growing into adulthood, and
change is a normal part of this transition.
University life can be exciting but also
overwhelming. You may notice shifts in your
student’s behaviour or emotions as they
adjust. By keeping communication open and
showing understanding, support and trust,
you can help your student navigate this new
stage with confidence.

Learning & Teaching
AAA Pedagogy   
Digital teaching and
Blended learning 
Our Triple A pedagogy (Active, Applied, and
Authentic), together with the University’s
Education Plan to 2028: Education for the Future
of Life and Work, has been scaled to ensure that
all learning is applied and all assessment is
authentic. The curriculum leverages AI and
emerging technologies and is aligned with industry
practices and tools to ensure relevance, currency,
and quality.  
The student experience at RMIT includes
classroom learning, facilitated online and self-
directed learning, peer learning, industry field
trips, and guest speakers. Additionally, students
gain critical life and work skills through our
Personal Edge program.  
Upon successful completion of their academic
journey, students receive a global RMIT degree.
All learning outcomes and assessments at RMIT
Vietnam are equivalent to those delivered at RMIT
Australia. Furthermore, we continuously monitor,
review and update our courses.  
Blended learning is an important part of how
students learn at RMIT. This approach combines
the best of both worlds: engaging face-to-face
classes and flexible online learning. It helps
students build strong academic skills while also
preparing them for the digital and professional
environments they will encounter in their future
careers.  
RMIT’s  blended learning is designed to be
accessible, inclusive, and well-connected.
Students learn in modern classrooms and
specialist facilities, supported by advanced digital
tools. Our core learning platform, Canvas, allows
students to access lessons, resources, and
assessments anytime. Other technologies such as
Microsoft Teams, Collaborate Ultra, and Turnitin
help create a seamless digital ecosystem where
students can communicate, collaborate, and learn
effectively. Together, these tools ensure that
students experience high-quality learning both on
campus and online, and are well prepared for the
demands of study, work, and life in a digital world.  
At RMIT, our teaching models are grounded in
international best practices to ensure students
acquire the skills needed for success in the
workplace. W e provide an industry-led and
student-centred learning experience.
Traditional exams have been replaced by
industry-relevant projects, and single-source
textbooks have given way to contemporary,
multimedia resources.

Results 
Students can view their course results in myRMIT - the student administration platform. To
respect student privacy, results are accessed directly by students. Many families find it helpful
to discuss progress together, with students choosing how and when to share their progress.
Grades
Academic progress
Academic progress is the formal way RMIT supports s tudents who are not making satisfactory
progress in their program. In general, students who have failed 50% or more of their enrolled
course load in a compulsory semester or teaching period; and/or who have failed a course for a
second time may move into a status of academic at risk. If this happens, the student will be
advised by email that they are at risk of not meeting the academic requirements of their
program. Students who are identified as being at the final stage at risk might be excluded from
their program for a full academic year. At risk students are encouraged to seek support from
Student Academic Success - SAS (refer to page 15). More details can be found in the Academic
Progress section of the RMIT website.

Familiarise yourself with some common RMIT
terms that your student may use when sharing

Word
Meaning
Assignment 
Assignments are tasks or projects assigned that students complete as part of their coursework.
These may include writing essays, conducting research, solving problems, or creating
presentations. Assignments help students apply what they have learned, demonstrate their
understanding, and contribute to their final grades. 
Academic
calendar 
An academic calendar outlines key dates for the academic year, including semester start and
end dates, public holidays, payment due dates, and other key dates. It helps students plan their
time effectively.
Assessment
rubric 
An assessment rubric is a guide that shows how a student’s work will be evaluated. The
assessment rubric outlines what skills or tasks the student needs to show, what good
performance looks like and different achievement levels. In short, rubrics help students
understand exactly what is expected before they start an assignment. 
Block-mode
courses 
A block-mode course is a study format where students focus on one course at a time over a
short, intensive period. Each block typically lasts 4–6 weeks, with more frequent or longer
classes. This allows students to focus deeply on one subject and receive quicker feedback on
their progress. 
 
Block-mode study can feel more intensive, but it helps students stay focused and manage
only one set of assessments at a time. Encouraging your child to plan ahead, keep up with
weekly tasks, and attend all classes will help them succeed.  
 
Students studying block-mode courses should also check their email regularly to avoid
missing important administrative deadlines, including census dates and payment deadlines
associated with block-mode study.
Census date 
The Census Date is the official deadline for students to finalise their enrolment in a course. It is
the last day students can withdraw from courses without being charged the full course fee.
After the Census Date, students are required to pay for the course(s), and the enrolled
course(s) information will be recorded on their academic record.
This is an important date for students to review and finalize their study load in a semester and
ensure their enrolment details are correct.
How to Speak RMIT
Consultations 
Consultations are scheduled times when students can meet with their lecturer outside of class to
ask questions and receive additional support.
Consultations may be held one-on-one or in small groups and provide a valuable opportunity for
personalised guidance. During these sessions, students can seek feedback on assignments,
clarify concepts they find challenging, and ask for academic advice. Students can email their
lecturers to arrange a consultation, subject to the lecturer’s availability and working hours.
Students are encouraged to attend consultations regularly, as they can significantly improve
understanding and confidence in the course.
